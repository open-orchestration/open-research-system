# Findings — RAG & Retrieval Architectures

**Question:** What does this category teach for building an AI research system?

## Key claims (cited)
- Naive RAG (embed docs → vector-search the raw query → dump top-K into the LLM) is the right place to *start* and the wrong place to *stop*: a 30-minute weekend demo works, but in production "recall is mediocre, citations are wrong, the model hallucinates around the gaps in retrieval." — [Your RAG Is Lying to You: 7 Failure Modes + 2026 Production Patterns](https://www.teacherandtask.com/blog/advanced-rag-patterns-2026-production-engineering-guide)
- Hybrid search (dense vectors + sparse BM25) is the single highest-ROI upgrade because dense handles paraphrase/synonyms while sparse catches exact terms, SKUs, product codes, and rare proper nouns embeddings miss; benchmarks show recall 0.72→0.91 and precision 0.68→0.87 vs BM25 alone. — [RAG Techniques Compared: A Practical Guide](https://blog.starmorph.com/blog/rag-techniques-compared-best-practices-guide)
- Reranking with a cross-encoder (which reads query+document *together*, unlike the bi-encoder used for first-pass retrieval) is the second highest-impact addition: retrieve 50–100 candidates, then re-score each (query, candidate) pair; hybrid+rerank improves precision ~25–40% over naive RAG at modest latency/cost. — [Your RAG Is Lying to You](https://www.teacherandtask.com/blog/advanced-rag-patterns-2026-production-engineering-guide)
- Chunking quality bounds everything downstream: optimized semantic chunking reaches 0.79–0.82 faithfulness vs 0.47–0.51 for naive fixed-size chunking — roughly a 70% improvement from chunking strategy alone. — [RAG Techniques Compared: A Practical Guide](https://blog.starmorph.com/blog/rag-techniques-compared-best-practices-guide)
- RAG's epistemic value is the "answer only from retrieved documents, with citations" constraint, which yields fewer hallucinations and verifiable answers, plus side benefits: per-user permissions enforced at the retrieval step, and a small model + good RAG often beating a large model alone on cost. — [The Complete Guide to RAG: Naive, Advanced, and Graph RAG in One](https://www.mrlatte.net/en/research/2026/04/27/rag-complete-guide/)
- Don't over-engineer: an agentic RAG pipeline can cost ~10x and add ~5s latency over naive RAG, so prove naive RAG is insufficient on *your actual data* before adding complexity — and even Cohere Rerank showed "no notable advantage over naive RAG" in the ARAGOG benchmark on some corpora, so benchmark on your own data. — [12 Advanced RAG Techniques Beyond Naive Retrieval [2026]](https://atlan.com/know/advanced-rag-techniques/)
- ColBERT-style late interaction is a deliberate middle ground — near cross-encoder precision at near dense-retrieval latency — and the highest-accuracy configurations stack three-way hybrid (Dense + BM25 + SPLADE) with a ColBERT reranker. — [Enterprise RAG Architecture: A Practitioner's Guide](https://www.applied-ai.com/briefings/enterprise-rag-architecture/)
- Infrastructure can stay simple longer than vendors admit: PostgreSQL + pgvector handles datasets under ~5M vectors with heavy metadata filtering, favoring operational simplicity over raw millisecond performance. — [Enterprise RAG Architecture: A Practitioner's Guide](https://www.applied-ai.com/briefings/enterprise-rag-architecture/)

## Convergent vs contested
- **Convergent:** A clear maturity ladder — Naive → Advanced → Graph/Modular RAG. Every source agrees the two highest-leverage, low-regret upgrades are **hybrid search** and **cross-encoder reranking**, that chunking quality dominates outcomes, and that the "answer only from retrieved + cite" constraint is what buys faithfulness.
- **Contested / open:** The value of heavier techniques is corpus-dependent — one benchmark found state-of-the-art RAG answers only 63% of factual questions (vs 44% for straightforward retrieval), while another found a commercial reranker gave "no notable advantage." Agentic RAG and GraphRAG are presented as situational (worth the 10x cost only for specific workloads), not universal upgrades.

## Implications for the system (Phase 2)
- Default the retrieval layer to **hybrid (dense + BM25) + cross-encoder rerank over 50–100 candidates**, and invest first in **semantic chunking** — these are the evidence-backed, low-regret wins before any graph/agentic complexity.
- Enforce the grounding contract end-to-end: answer only from retrieved chunks, attach citations per claim, and apply permission filtering at the retrieval step.
- Make complexity earn its place: start on pgvector, add GraphRAG/agentic retrieval only when a workload-specific eval (recall/precision/faithfulness on our own corpus) proves naive/advanced RAG insufficient.

## Gaps found → re-scan
- Coverage of hybrid/rerank/chunking is strong, but GraphRAG is named more than it is explained — no detail on entity-graph construction or community summaries for global/holistic queries, and no concrete agentic-RAG loop design. Eval methodology (RAGAS, faithfulness/answer-relevance metrics) is referenced but not specified.
